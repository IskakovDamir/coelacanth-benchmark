# Annotator brief (benchmark v0)

For recruiting the two paid annotators, STEM students, who do the first pass on benchmark v0. Damir adjudicates their disagreements and checks a sample of their work. Written 2026-09-24 for the 100-item scope and revised on 2026-10-06 for the decided v0 of about 80 items (`docs/v0-composition.md`) and protocol v1.0-rc2. The time figures are estimates from the worked examples in `eval/benchmark/LABELING.md`; they are replaced by measured minutes after the first 10 timed items.

## The task

Coelacanth is a benchmark of cross-field rediscoveries: a claim published in one field, and earlier work in another field that states the same mechanism or result, often under another name. Each item is one claim plus the earlier candidate works that annotators and systems found. For each item the annotator:

1. reads the source work of the claim (the method or result section and the abstract);
2. writes the claim in the source field's own words (`claim.text`) and in neutral words (`claim.skeleton`), or, on an item the other annotator wrote, checks both against the source;
3. checks the claim chart that Claude pre-filled: two to six elements, and which of them are key;
4. for each candidate, checks the pre-filled quotes element by element against both texts, confirms whether the candidate comes from a different field than the claim, then picks one of six relation labels with the ordered decision rules (`equivalent`, `specialization`, `generalization`, `partial`, `similar_mechanism`, `different`);
5. records confidence (1 to 3), attribution (does the claim's source cite the candidate?) and minutes spent.

Rules that matter most:
- Humans choose every label and confirm every cross-field flag. Claude's pre-fill (charts, candidate evidence, quotes) is checked, corrected where wrong, and disclosed in the datasheet.
- Keep item files confidential until release (agreement, section 2). The Russian documents carry no instruction about AI tools (Damir, 7 Oct: the annotators are people he knows who do not use AI, and warnings read as distrust); that labels are the annotator's own rests on the agreement's section 1.
- Work blind: no system outputs, no closed-book results, no other annotator's labels before adjudication. Candidates pooled from systems arrive with no mark of which system found them.
- When unsure between two labels, take the one that claims less prior art and set confidence to 1. A false "already done" is the most expensive error.
- Everything stays confidential until the public release (target 30 Oct 2026).

Training: read LABELING.md (about 3,600 words), label three of its worked examples without looking at the answers, compare, discuss. About 3 hours.

## Setup

- Benchmark v0: about 80 labelled items: about 42 rediscovery positives, 8 attributed Kulikowski imports as controls (they test that a system does not flag honest, cited reuse), 10 methods generated by AI models, and about 20 negatives (10 made by the annotators from a real positive by changing one key element, 10 natural near-misses).
- Every item is labelled in full by two annotators, independently (raised on 7 Oct from second readings of the decision-critical fields; Damir: do not cut the workload). With two annotators each labels all 80 items; with more, `python -m coelacanth.benchmark.assign` rotates the pairs. The 25 first items of b01 and b02's 12 crossover items stay with a1 and a2 for the reliability measurement.
- Damir settles every disagreement with a one-line reason, checks the evidence of every `equivalent` and `specialization` label, and decides every AI-origin item.
- After the systems run, the annotator who labelled an item also judges the candidates the systems found for it, in one sitting: title and abstract first, then the claim chart for the close ones, blind to which system found what. Damir adjudicates every pooled candidate rated `similar_mechanism` or stronger; the other annotator re-screens a random 10% of the candidates screened out (decision 17).
- Agreement is measured on the 25 shared items around 12 Oct. If it falls short (Krippendorff's alpha under 0.80 on "already done" or on gold, under 0.667 on the six-way label), LABELING.md is revised once and agreement is measured again on 12 fresh shared items (decision 18).

## Time per item (estimate)

Per-step minutes, central value with a low and a high value. The worked examples set the unit counts: their claim charts have 2 to 4 elements (mean 3.0), and each labelled pair needs up to two quotes per covered element (one from each text).

| Step | Central | Low | High | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Read the source work | 10 | 5 | 20 | one claim per item |
| Write `claim.text` and `claim.skeleton` | 8 | 5 | 12 | at most 120 words each |
| Check them on a shared item | 3 | 2 | 5 | |
| Check the other annotator's claim text on a single-annotated item | 5 | 3 | 10 | read the relevant part of the source |
| Check the pre-filled chart | 3 | 2 | 5 | about 1 minute per element, 3 elements |
| Judge one candidate in depth, including its cross-field flag | 8 | 5 | 12 | 3 elements: 1 minute per pre-filled quote pair, 3 minutes for the decision rules and the record |
| Candidates judged in depth per open-search positive | 2 | 1 | 3 | the declared earlier work plus one or two close ones |
| Candidates judged in depth per Kulikowski control | 2.3 | 2 | 3.5 | his 210 twin pairs over 92 queries give about 4.6 twins per query, about half of them earlier |
| Candidates judged in depth per negative | 1 | 1 | 1 | the closest candidate the searches returned |
| Screen one pooled candidate from title and abstract | 1 | 1 | 1.5 | most are `different`, which needs no evidence |
| Unjudged pooled candidates per open-search item | 20 | 10 | 40 | top 10 of about six systems, in two conditions, with overlap; measured on the first pooled items |

First pass, central values: about 39 minutes for a Kulikowski control, 37 for an open-search positive, 29 for a negative; a second pass on a shared item about 30 minutes.

## Hours for about 80 items

Mix: 42 rediscovery positives and 10 AI-origin items at the open-search rate, 8 Kulikowski controls, 20 negatives. The first pass and its low and high values are scaled from the 100-item estimate by that mix; pooled screening covers all 80 items.

| Work | Central hours | Low | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full labelling, 80 items, twice (each item by two annotators) | 93.8 | 48.2 | 179.8 |
| Pooled screening, 80 items (20 candidates at 1 minute; 10 to 40 at 1 to 1.5) | 26.7 | 13.3 | 80.0 |
| Re-screening 10% of screened-out candidates | 2.4 | 2.4 | 2.4 |
| Training, two annotators | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| **Total, both annotators** | **128.9** | **69.9** | **268.2** |
| **Per annotator** | **64.5** | **35.0** | **134.1** |

The annotators' own search adds about 10 to 20 minutes on an ordinary item and up to 45 on AI-origin items and items where nothing close turns up; the central estimate counts only part of it.

Damir: about 7 hours to adjudicate the 80 doubly labelled items (5 minutes each) and check the `equivalent` and `specialization` evidence, about 5 hours for the 10 AI-origin items (30 minutes each), and the pooled candidates rated `similar_mechanism` or stronger (measured in round 1).

## Pay

At $8 to $10 per hour, paid hourly with minutes logged per item (so hard items are not rushed), each annotator costs about $520 to $640 at the central estimate ($280 to $1,340 across the low and high cases); both together about $1,030 to $1,290. This is outside the $500 October cap for API runs.
