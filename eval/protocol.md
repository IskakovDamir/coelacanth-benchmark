# Evaluation protocol (v1.0 release candidate, not frozen)

Version 1.0-rc3, 2026-10-06 (rc1 and rc2 the same day; drafts v0.1 to v0.3 of 2026-09-24; history in section 14), with the pre-freeze updates of 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-09 recorded in section 14. Status: release candidate. The rules freeze on the day the annotation files are sent to the annotators (decision 3), after the model documentation is re-read (section 2): the frozen text gets the git tag `protocol-v1.0` and its SHA-256 is printed in the paper. After freezing, every change is logged in section 14 and nothing is changed silently (CLAUDE.md).

Scope of v1 (ECIR 2027): benchmark v0, measurement A (existing checkers) and, if it passes its readiness checks, the reference pipeline in its single-agent configuration. The ACL comparison (multi-agent vs single agent at equal budget) gets its own frozen version, v2, before its runs (section 10).

## 1. Data and subsets

- **Items**: benchmark v0 as defined in `eval/benchmark/SCHEMA.md`, labelled with `eval/benchmark/LABELING.md`: about 80 adjudicated items (`docs/v0-composition.md`): about 42 rediscovery positives (equivalence declarations, Kulikowski's twins whose source does not name the method, catalogue rediscoveries, post-cutoff leads, the non-computational controls with a known unification), at most 10 attributed Kulikowski controls (subset `C`), 10 to 12 AI-origin items (stratum `A`) and about 20 negatives (decision 14). v0 grows toward 100 only if at least 50 rediscoveries pass the two-reading hand-check standard by 14 Oct. The paper reports the number adjudicated, never a target. The candidate queue is `data/candidates/queue.md`. Only adjudicated items are scored (decision 4).
- **Gold**: for each item, the candidates with `gold: true` (adjudicated relation `similar_mechanism` or stronger) within the judged pool (section 12), and the adjudicated item verdict.
- **Cross-field gold**: gold candidates whose cross-field status a person confirmed (`candidates[].cross_field: true`, with `candidates[].field_confirmed_by`). OpenAlex fields are only a suggestion; they are unreliable for old papers.
- **Subsets**, fixed on 19 Oct before any scored system run and stored in `splits` with a SHA-256:
  - `ALL`: every adjudicated item.
  - `R` (the inferential set): adjudicated rediscovery positives (not `C`, not `A`) with at least one person-confirmed cross-field gold work. Target: at least 30 adjudicated `R` items, labelled before AI-origin items and negatives (`docs/design/h-decision.md`, change 7). Stratifier **recalled**: a closed-book sample of any probed model, under either prompt, names a cross-field gold work (section 3), decided by a person before any system run. The confirmatory tests and the main estimates are on `R`, each also given per recall stratum, descriptively (section 8).
  - `H`: the `R` items that are not recalled: no closed-book sample of any probed model, under either prompt, names a cross-field gold work, whatever the item's date (section 3). Described as "no closed-book recall detected under two probes", never as uncontaminated or unseen. Reported first in every headline table, descriptively: k of n with a Wilson 95% interval, never tested (decision 23). `H` has no target or floor (the rc3 target of 25 and floor of 20 are retired, section 14). `H_A` (no frozen-prompt sample names a cross-field gold work) appears only as a labelled sensitivity line. Composition guard: if fewer than 60% of the first 30 labelled rediscovery positives fail closed-book for every model, further supply shifts away from well-known Kulikowski families.
  - `H-any`: `H` items on which no sample names any gold work. Reported alongside.
  - `H-post`: the `H` items that are post-cutoff for every model the compared systems call (section 2). Descriptive below 20 items, with its source composition; never read as a contamination effect. The AI-origin stratum `A` is post-cutoff by construction and is reported on its own, not added to `H-post`.
  - `H+C`: `H` plus the closed-book-failed controls, a secondary line. `H-final` (pooled gold) and `H-concept` (concept names count) are robustness lines.
  - `K` (Kulikowski closed pool, section 5), `C` (attributed controls), `A` (AI-origin stratum), `N` (negatives, by adjudicated verdict).
  - `P40`: decision 2 (drawn from `R`).
- Every headline table opens with `H`'s k of n; the headline estimates and the confirmatory tests are on `R`, split by recall (on `P40` for Theorizer, Owl and Hunch), and `H`'s estimates follow as descriptive rows whatever they show; an advantage on `R` that `H` does not show is stated as such (section 8). `ALL` numbers are reported too, labelled as such.
- Every result is also reported without the items our own pipeline mined (`provenance.mined_by` starting with `our_pipeline:`) and, for Hunch, without items whose pools came from its own earlier lists.
- What `H` selects: the gold works' OpenAlex citation counts are reported for `R` items in and out of `H`; `H` is enriched for long-tail links, so its scores favour retrieval over parametric knowledge.

## 2. Post-cutoff

An item is post-cutoff for model M when `claim.date` falls after the end of the month that follows M's training-data cutoff month. The cutoff is the latest date the provider's documentation gives for M's training data (the later of the training-data and reliable-knowledge cutoffs). Cells stay blank where the documentation gives nothing; nothing is guessed.

## 3. Closed-book runs

- **Probed models**: Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 4.5. `H` and the recalled stratum use all three (decision 8).
- For every positive item (rediscoveries, `C`, `A`) and every probed model, run the model without tools on the field-native `claim.text` and `claim.date` (never on `claim.skeleton`), with this prompt: *If this idea or result was published before the given date, name the earliest publications you know that state it: title, authors, year, field. If you do not know, answer "unknown".* The user message adds *List at most five publications, one per line.*; the system prompt is *Answer from your own knowledge. You have no tools and cannot look anything up.* (`src/coelacanth/closedbook.py`).
- Five samples per item and model, default temperature, each model's default effort (medium for Opus 5.5, high for Sonnet 5.5; Haiku 4.5 has no effort setting), no hard cap on output tokens. Each item is probed as soon as its claim text is checked; answers are stored and scored later, so they can be re-scored at no cost.
- Calls: the Claude Code CLI in headless mode (`--bare` and `--safe-mode`: no login, keychain, CLAUDE.md, hooks, plugins or MCP servers) with no tools, an API key from `.env`, a neutral working directory, model fallback disabled and answers from another model rejected; the CLI version is recorded per call. Cost per call is priced from `modelUsage` at the official prices.
- **Second probe** (cross-field framing): *Has this method or result appeared earlier in a different field, possibly under a different name? If so, name the earliest publications: title, authors, year, field. If you do not know, answer "unknown".* Its rule, fixed before its first call on the 20 pilot items: if it names cross-field gold for an item the frozen prompt missed, `H` requires failing both probes and the second probe runs on all positives; otherwise it is reported as checked and dropped. On 7 Oct it caught 8 of the 9 pilot items the frozen prompt missed (`eval/results/closed-book-second-probe/`), so it runs on every positive at 3 samples per model, with the other settings above, and `H` requires failing both probes.
- **Hit**: a sample names a gold candidate. Matching is automatic first (title overlap, author and year); every loose match, every strict match below 95% title overlap and every overruled match goes to a person, before any system run and blind to model identity, with this rubric: yes if the line gives the title (minor errors allowed) with first author or year, or the first author with a distinctive title fragment, venue or book; author and year alone count only if OpenAlex lists no other work by that first author within a year either side on the topic; years count within one; variant publications of the same work count; the claim's own source, another work of the same author, a concept or an eponym without a year do not. A second person re-decides a random 30 of these; agreement and the number of `H` flips are reported, the first decision standing.
- Before `H` is computed, every work named in an item's closed-book answers that resolves in OpenAlex and predates `claim.date` joins its candidates, judged blind like any candidate, unless the item's gold in hand already puts it outside `H`; the number of named works left unjudged is reported (decision 1).
- **Declined samples**: decision 6.
- **Recalled** (stratifies `R`): at least one sample of any probed model, under either prompt, hits a cross-field gold candidate. **Closed-book-failed** (defines `H`): no sample under either prompt hits any cross-field gold candidate. `H_A` (sensitivity line only): no frozen-prompt sample hits a cross-field gold candidate. `H-any`: no sample hits any gold candidate. Negatives have no gold and are not probed for these splits.
- Cost is booked on the PLAN.md line for pilots and closed-book runs: about $14 at pilot prices for 70 positives and three models (`reference/research-notes/method-contamination.md`), each run under the $20 single-run limit.

## 4. Leakage controls

- `claim.text` is field-native: the claim as the source work states it, in the source field's own vocabulary, with author names (eponyms included), titles, years, venues and the coined method name removed, and nothing else changed (SCHEMA.md rule 5). `claim.skeleton`, the neutral version, is never shown to systems except in the vocabulary-barrier ablation (section 13). Claude-drafted claim texts also pass a vocabulary-leak check: content words absent from the source's cached text are listed, and those found in a gold candidate's text are resolved by the claim-text checker before labelling.
- **Leakage rule**: any retrieved work or evidence dated on or after `claim.date` is a leakage hit, whatever its kind: papers, preprints, encyclopedia pages and other web pages included. A web page counts by the date of the version the system read; undated evidence counts as a leakage hit. A leakage hit never counts as a correct find, and the leakage rate is reported per system (section 7).
- Versions of one work are resolved to its earliest public version before the leakage rule is applied; gold candidates are dated before `claim.date` by construction (SCHEMA.md rule 6).
- Systems we run get a date filter (before `claim.date`) and the `exclusions` list. For services we cannot filter (Owl, Theorizer, web-searching agents), leakage and exclusion hits are removed afterwards and counted.
- Worked examples from LABELING.md are never benchmark items. Annotators' search logs are not shown to any system, and annotators keep item files confidential until release (annotator agreement, section 2).
- Release: decision 26.

## 5. Closed-pool protocol (Kulikowski items)

- Pool: the 109 papers of Kulikowski's curated benchmark (package tag v1.0.0, `eval/results/kulikowski-baseline/`). Queries: the 92 member papers that have a twin in the pool. Candidates for a query: the other papers from a different field (his `reproduce.py` definition: cross-field pairs only; 5,812 pairs, 210 twins).
- Input text: the package's abstract-only files (496 bundled plus five rebuilt through OpenAlex; the rebuild differs from the frozen bytes, documented in the baseline README). Full text (condition B) is not run in v1.
- A system ranks all cross-field candidates for each query. Metrics: P@1 and MRR per query (92 queries); AP over the 5,812 cross-field pairs from symmetric pair scores (the mean of the two directed scores; systems that return only ranks use score = 1 / rank).
- Baselines from the reproduction: whole fingerprint + TF-IDF AP 0.557 [0.407, 0.720]; skeleton P@1 0.815; whole fingerprint P@1 0.826 with unigram TF-IDF (0.870 with the headline TF-IDF settings, from our diagnostic).

## 7. Metrics

- **Primary** (decision 5): cross-field success@10 per item, reported as the cross-field miss rate, k of n, with Wilson 95% intervals.
- **Retrieval, secondary**: cross-field recall@10, MRR@10, success@10 and recall@10 over all gold, success@1; recall@50 only as a lower bound with judged@50, in the appendix. Leakage hits are removed from the ranked list first; they never count as finds.
- **Verdict**: three-class macro-F1 (already done = `equivalent` or `specialization`; related = `generalization`, `partial`, `similar_mechanism`; `nothing_close`) on `R` plus `N`, split by recall, with the `H` rows first (section 1); the six-class confusion matrix, descriptive; already-done precision separately; the false already-done rate over every item whose adjudicated verdict is weaker than `specialization`, overall and per stratum.
- **Leakage** (per system): share of items with at least one leakage hit in the top 10 or in the evidence; leakage hits per item; the share naming a gold relation. For the memory reference (section 6), named works dated on or after `claim.date` are parametric leakage, reported the same way.
- **Pool**: judged-pool depth, pool size per item, unjudged@10 for systems scored after qrels v1, and the four bias checks of section 12.
- **Vocabulary gap** (per system): each metric on `claim.skeleton` minus the same metric on `claim.text` (section 13).
- **Calibration** (systems with probabilities): Brier score, ECE with 10 equal-width bins, reliability diagram.
- **Grounding**: share of quotes passing the verbatim check (the reference pipeline must reach 100%); share of 50 sampled quotes a person judges to support the claimed element.
- **Efficiency, budget-aware** (per system): items run, mean and maximum dollars per item, input and output tokens from `modelUsage` (tool calls included), web searches, seconds, items stopped by the cap, price date; a cross-field success@10 against cost figure. Systems run at different budgets are not ranked against each other.
- **Human levels**: annotator agreement and annotator search recall, and random and majority baselines, next to the systems.

## 11. Human annotation

- Two paid annotators, STEM students recruited with `docs/annotator-brief.md` ($8 to $10 per hour, outside the $500 API cap), do the first pass; the 25 first items of b01 are shared. Adjudication: decision 4. Late annotators: decision 19.
- Reliability (decision 18): Krippendorff's alpha, nominal, with bootstrap 95% intervals and the probability of alpha falling below 0.667 and 0.800, on independent first readings only; Cohen's kappa, raw agreement and the confusion matrix alongside; four distinctions: six-way verdict, already done vs not, gold vs not per candidate, cross-field flag on gold. Measured on the 25 shared b01 items once both annotators have labelled them; a failing distinction triggers one logged LABELING.md revision and a second measurement on fresh shared items (from b03, since annotators receive b01 and b02 together: section 14), never on the discussed ones. The final reliability covers every item with two readings (contrast items excepted, LABELING.md section 5). Pooled judgments report positive specific agreement on gold.
- Pre-fill (decision 25): Claude may pre-fill the claim text, skeleton, chart, candidate evidence and quotes; humans choose every label (pair relations, key flags, item verdict) and confirm every gold candidate's cross-field status. Notes annotators see state facts only, enforced by `check-packet`; Claude's assessments stay in the hidden meta for the adjudicator. Pre-filled claim texts are drafts (`claim.written_by: prefill:claude`) until an annotator checks them against the source (`claim.checked_by`). The pre-fill is recorded per item and disclosed in the datasheet; b01 agreement is reported as agreement under a shared pre-fill.
- Anchor (decision 25), measured by `python -m coelacanth.benchmark.anchor` (`src/coelacanth/benchmark/anchor.py`) on the independent first readings that agreement uses; report in `eval/results/anchor/<batches>-anchor.json`, its table beside it in `<batches>-anchor.txt`; every number descriptive. For every done reading on a pre-filled packet, against the draft that reading was made on: the share of changed words in `claim.text` and in `claim.skeleton`, and the chart elements removed, added and edited (matched by element id, texts only, since the draft carries no key flags). A word is a whitespace-separated token after the quote checker's normalisation, with leading and trailing punctuation removed and case kept; the share of changed words is the word-level Levenshtein distance (insertion, deletion or substitution of one word, each costing 1) divided by the word count of the longer text, 0 when both are empty. For each of the 12 b02 crossover items (`b02-plan.json`, groups X and Y): the verdict of the reading with the draft against that of the reading without it, reported as k of n with a Wilson 95% interval (section 8: under 20 items, no test), next to both readings' claim-text shares against the draft (for the bare reading, the draft its annotator never saw: the reference for how far an independently written text lands) and both readings' minutes. As a reference on other items, the verdict agreement of every pair of readings made on the same draft. An item lacking either reading is listed with the reason and left out of k and n.
- Annotators label in the offline tool `tools/annotate.html`; batches carry no `found_by`, provenance or closed-book results; `python -m coelacanth.benchmark.annotation check-labels` validates the export and merges it into items that pass the loader.
- Annotators work blind to system outputs, closed-book results and which system found a pooled candidate.

## 12. Pooling

- TREC-style pooling at depth 10, equal to the depth of the headline metrics: for each item, the top 10 candidates of every system under test (both conditions of section 13 where they run) join the annotators' own candidates and the closed-book-named works (decision 1). No adaptive or bandit selection of what to judge; a relevance density above one third in an item's judged pool is a diagnostic flag only.
- Automatic exclusions, counted and reported, with 20 checked by a person: works dated on or after `claim.date`, duplicate versions, citations that resolve to no work.
- Judging: decision 17. Items are adjudicated only when no candidate within the recorded depth is unjudged (SCHEMA.md rule 10). A candidate outside the judged pool counts as not gold.
- Relevance judgments are versioned: qrels v0 before pooling, qrels v1 after round 2, frozen before any score is computed. Systems scored after qrels v1 report three lines: the standard score, the condensed-list score (a bound) and unjudged@10; results with mean unjudged@10 above 1 are flagged, and their unjudged top 10 are judged blind when possible (qrels v1.x, every system rescored).
- Bias checks, pre-registered: leave-own-group-out (both conditions of a system as one group; the authors' two systems as one group; the memory reference as its own group), reported as items flipped and as the mean and maximum change in recall@10, flagged above 5% relative or for any flipped item; the share of gold found by exactly one group; `shared_key_terms` of pooled gold vs annotator and mined gold; the annotators' own unique gold.
- Items mined with our own pipeline are marked in `provenance.mined_by`; every result is reported with and without them (section 1).

## 13. Vocabulary-barrier ablation

- Each system runs twice per item, same date and settings: on the field-native `claim.text` (the main condition) and on `claim.skeleton`. The gap measures the vocabulary barrier.
- Coverage (decisions 22 and 34): both conditions for the reference pipeline, the re-implemented baselines and the single agent on all of `R`, funded as tier T3 (section 9); Theorizer, Owl and Hunch only if their lines leave room after the main condition.
- Both conditions feed the pool (depth 10 for the skeleton runs if the measured judging hours allow, else depth 5 with judged@10 shown); the closed-book split uses the field-native text only. Pools of items outside `R` lack skeleton runs, and the paper says so.
